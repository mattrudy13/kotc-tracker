Are the percentages honest?

How it works

Minutes. A weighted average of the player's recent minutes this season (a game's weight halves every 6 games back), with last season's average counting as one extra game. Players ruled out on ESPN's injury report are dropped; questionable or day-to-day players get a 70% chance to play.

PRA per minute. The same recent weighting, pulled toward last season's rate (worth 100 minutes of this season) and, for players with little history, toward a league-wide 0.78.

Game. Projected PRA = minutes × PRA per minute, scaled up or down with the betting total (a high total means more possessions). A blowout adjustment for big spreads was tested and made the projections worse, so it's off.

Simulation. Each night is played out 10,000 times. Every player's PRA varies around the projection (the spread was measured from how far 2025-26 projections actually missed), and a shared game factor moves everyone in a fast or overtime game together. Win = the share of simulations a player finished first; ties count as a split.

Fitting and testing. The settings were chosen using October–January 2025-26 only, then tested untouched on February–April. The replay only ever sees games before each night, with one shortcut: it knows who actually played (standing in for the final injury report), so it never has to guess about late scratches. Live predictions do have to guess, so expect them to do a bit worse.

What it ignores (for now): teammates being out (more shots for everyone else), the opponent's defense, back-to-backs, and how many people pick each player, which sets the payout. See the notes on the History page.
